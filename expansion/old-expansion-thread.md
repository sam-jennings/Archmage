## 1. Should you add an offensive spell?

Short answer: **yes, but only as a *separate, opt-in expansion*** with explicit warnings.

Why it’s viable:
- You already have a strong fiction: *magic is structure*. Structure can be disrupted.
- Advanced players often want *interaction that matters*, not just racing.
- It gives the Cabal / co-op sets another lever **without relying on randomness**.

Why it’s risky:
- Your game currently has **no irreversible player-on-player harm**
- Destruction creates emotional spikes that may outweigh strategic value
- At low player counts, offense easily becomes kingmaking

So: this must be a **late-stage, expert-only module**, clearly framed as such.

---

## 2. Hard design constraints (non-negotiable)

Any offensive spell **must obey all of these**:

### 1. No full destruction
- You may not delete a spell
- You may not remove more than **one component** per cast
- You may not target the same spell twice in a round

### 2. No surprise timing
- Offensive spells are cast **only during the caster’s turn**
- Never during another player’s action
- Never as a reaction

### 3. No value manipulation
- You may not change card values
- You may not swap values between players
- Structure can change; numbers are sacred

### 4. Must cost *real tempo*
- Casting offense must:
  - Consume a full cast
  - And/or discard to Reserve
  - And/or advance Drought pressure

If offense is “free”, it becomes dominant.

---

## 3. Three viable offensive spell models

### MODEL A — **Disjunction** (structural erosion, safest)

**Concept:** You don’t destroy magic—you *separate* it.

#### Spell definition
> **Disjunction**  
> Pattern: Any valid transmutation of **exactly 3 components**  
> Effect: Remove **one edge** from another player’s spell (splitting it into two spells).

Key points:
- You are not removing cards
- You are not touching values
- You are converting *one* spell into *two weaker spells*

#### Why this works
- It punishes over-concentration
- It creates interesting rebuild decisions
- It doesn’t erase progress

#### Value / resonance integration
- If the targeted spell crosses **two resonance bands**, you may choose *which* edge breaks.
- If it’s entirely within one band, the defender chooses.

This makes coherent spells more resilient.

---

### MODEL B — **Dissonance** (value-aware degradation)

**Concept:** Magic fails when frequencies clash.

#### Spell definition
> **Dissonance**  
> Pattern: A spell containing **two non-adjacent values**  
> Effect: Target a spell. If its **highest and lowest values differ by ≥ X**, remove the *outermost* component.

This introduces a *numerical vulnerability* without randomness.

#### Why this works
- High-volatility spells become fragile
- Low-band and tight-band strategies gain defensive value
- Players self-balance their designs

#### Resonance interaction
- Entirely single-band spells:
  - Defender chooses which end is removed
- Multi-band spells:
  - Attacker chooses

This cleanly rewards resonance coherence.

---

### MODEL C — **Unravelling** (Reserve-based, highest interaction)

**Concept:** External magic cannot sustain complexity.

#### Spell definition
> **Unravelling**  
> Pattern: Any transmutation of 4+ components  
> Cost: Discard 2 to Reserve  
> Effect: Choose a spell. For each of its components beyond 3, the owner must discard 1 card from hand **or** remove 1 component (their choice).

This introduces **pressure, not certainty**.

#### Why this works
- Defender retains agency
- Reserve becomes strategically relevant
- Big spells remain possible but costly to defend

#### Value integration
- If the attacking spell is in a *higher resonance band* than the target, the defender discards before choosing.
- If lower, the defender chooses freely.
