# Expansion Directions — Echo and the High Frequencies

**Premise of this exploration:** the expansion line consists of exactly two things — **Ascension Trials** (endgame scoring depth) and **Spell Duels** (in-game conflict, tiered). The Living Array and The Conclave are deliberately excluded from this document's thinking. The question: starting from that fixed pair, what else does the design support — specifically directions that spend the two parked design-space assets, the **Echo current** (fifth energy) and **16+ value components**, which exist precisely to widen what expansions can do?

**The Catan test applied throughout:** each direction must own a mechanical throughline the others don't (Seafarers = exploration, Cities & Knights = upgrading, Traders & Barbarians = scenarios). The existing pair already owns two throughlines — Trials owns *how the game scores*, Spell Duels owns *how players collide*. Anything new must own a third.

**Complexity anchors used below:** Trials ≈ medium (all its weight is at evaluation); Spell Duels spans low (Tier 1) to very high (Tier 5), with its expected product somewhere in the middle.

Everything below is modular and opt-in. Nothing changes the base four-energy core loop; Echo and 16+ components only ever enter the game when their module is in the box.

---

## Direction 1 — The Fifth Current
### Throughline: *widening* — the magic itself grows a new voice

**Hook.** A current no wizard has ever attuned begins to sing through the failing world — **Echo**, the resonance of magic answering itself. The deck gains a full fifth suit; every pattern in the game gets wider, and mastery gets a new summit: the five-part harmony.

**What it is mechanically.** Echo components (values 1–15 / 1–20, matching the deck in use) shuffle into the Source. No new verbs, no new phases. What changes is pattern space:

- **Matching values get easier** (five chances per rank instead of four) — which is exactly the relief the enchantment economy has always wanted, delivered by *supply* instead of by rules patches.
- **The enchantment ladder replaces the binary cliff** (per STATE.md's parked Stage-4+ note): 3-match = +1 counter, 4-match = +2 counters, **5-match = Unlimited with a Drought cap** — the 4-card "Unlimited, tension collapses" problem dissolves into a graded climb whose top rung is genuinely legendary.
- **Trials integration:** a fifth energy domain (Echo Power), and Purity/Harmony/Complete Mastery stretch across five currents; one new trial — **The Quintessence** (power from all five energy domains) — slots into the existing 7th-trial pattern.
- **6–7 player support** stops leaning on a thin 1–20 stretch and gets real card volume instead.

**Complexity vs. the pair:** *below* both. Lighter than any Spell Duels tier above 1, far lighter than Trials — there is nothing to teach except "there's a fifth colour and the ladder." Its weight is invisible: it's all in rebalancing (deck ratios, Array odds, score table, every Trials pool).

**What Echo specifically enables that four energies can't:** the 5-match rank, the graded Unlimited, and five-current trial geometry are arithmetically impossible at four suits — you cannot ladder to a fifth rung that doesn't exist. This is the *only* direction that needs Echo to be a true, full peer current (the others use Echo as a special-role suit).

**Why it's distinct and worth building:** it isn't interaction (Duels) and isn't scoring allocation (Trials) — it's the *substrate* both stand on, made wider. It's also the closest thing here to a Catan **extension** (more players, same game) wearing expansion clothes; honest framing on the box matters. Worth building because half its design is already specified in STATE.md's parked note — it's the cheapest of the four to reach testable.

## Direction 2 — Echoes of the Spent
### Throughline: *the endgame becomes an act* — the Drought stops being a wind-down

**Hook.** Echo is the resonance of magic **already used**. As the living currents die, the spent ones begin to answer. Echo components live not in the Source but in the **Arcane Reserve's** orbit — and when the Drought comes, they wake. The Drought stops being a quiet optimisation coda and becomes the game's third act, and the wizards who prepared for the world *after* magic inherit it.

**What it is mechanically (sketch).**

- Echo components enter play only via the Reserve: seeded into it at setup, and earned during normal play by **resonance-binding** — when you Unlearn or discard to the Reserve, matching Echo values may come back to you (the spent magic answering its own frequency).
- Spells containing an Echo anchor (1+ Echo component) are **castable during the Drought** — the only casting the Drought has ever allowed. Effects are Drought-scaled analogues (draw from the Released Reserve instead of the Source, etc.).
- The Released Reserve becomes a played space rather than a blind pile — Echo lets you *hear* it (peek/filter effects).
- New scoring dimension: what your Spellbook *did* during the Drought, not just what it holds at evaluation.

**Complexity vs. the pair:** roughly Trials-weight, differently distributed — Trials front-loads nothing and detonates at scoring; this is quiet for two acts and detonates at the Drought. Above Spell Duels Tiers 1–2, below Tier 5.

**What Echo specifically enables:** the four base currents are *ambient* magic — they are definitionally what dies in the Drought; letting any of them cast through it would contradict the game's own fiction and every "Drought stays clean" rule. Only a fifth current defined as **spent magic resonating** can operate after the Source empties without breaking the base game's logic. No 16+ dependency (they'd slot in as rare high-value Echoes, optional).

**Why it's distinct and worth building:** Trials owns evaluation, Duels owns player collision — nothing owns **the Drought**, which is the game's title moment and currently its flattest stretch of play. Every playtest note that ever says "the ending is quieter than the build-up" is this expansion's market research. It's also the direction most likely to *sell* the theme: the pitch line writes itself ("the expansion where the ending fights back").

## Direction 3 — The High Mysteries
### Throughline: *vertical progression* — the Cities & Knights of the line

**Hook.** Beyond the twentieth frequency lie the **overtones** — resonances the Academy sealed because no living wizard could hold them. Components valued **16–20** (2–4p deck) / **21–25** (5–7p deck) exist outside the Source entirely, displayed as a face-up **Mystery ladder**. They cannot be drawn. They must be *earned* — and each one claimed permanently raises what a Spellbook can be.

**What it is mechanically (sketch).**

- A rack of overtone components, visible from setup, each tagged with a claim condition expressed in base-game verbs: *cast a 6+ Conjuration → claim the lowest Radiance overtone; complete a Perfect Transmutation → claim any overtone of its energy; hold three Enchantments → claim from any current;* etc. Claiming is the new race everyone can see.
- Overtones extend the pattern ceiling: sequences run past the old maximum (a 14-15-**16** Transfiguration; a PT running 17-18-19), matches reach into values no rank has, and **Masterwork** spells (any spell containing 2+ overtones) score on a new tier and carry weight in Trials (a natural 8th trial: *The Deep Resonance* — masterwork power only).
- Everything stays in base verbs: no new phase, no new action. The additions are the rack, the conditions, and the score tier.

**Complexity vs. the pair:** the heaviest — at or above Spell Duels Tier 4, and unlike Duels it can't tier down far (the rack, conditions, and score tier are one organism). This is the true **big-box**: the one that changes what a full game arcs toward, the way Cities & Knights turns settlements into a metropolis race.

**What 16+ specifically enables that the current caps can't:** the base value cap is load-bearing — every sequence, score row, and trial is tuned to 15/20, so *nothing inside the current range can ever be "beyond" it.* A prestige tier physically requires numbers that don't exist yet; that is literally what the 16+ design space was parked for. Echo is optional garnish here (overtone Echoes as the rarest claims) — this is the 16+ flagship, mirror to Direction 1 being the Echo flagship.

**Why it's distinct and worth building:** Duels is horizontal (against neighbours), Trials is retrospective (judging what you built) — nothing in the line is **aspirational during play**. The visible rack gives every game a public race and gives strong players a ceiling to chase, without touching interaction or scoring's existing owners. It's also the only direction that makes the *game's own numbers* the fantasy: the cards themselves are the treasure.

## Direction 4 — The Convergence
### Throughline: *the magic plays back* — co-op and solo

**Hook.** Before it chooses an Archmage, the dying magic **tests the whole Academy**. The Convergence is Archmage Ascension with the game itself as the opponent: the table wins together — stabilise the currents before the Source collapses — or the world's magic ends with no ascension at all.

**What it is mechanically (sketch).**

- An **Echo deck** acts as the world's voice: each round reveals a demand pattern — *"the currents require a 4-length Flux sequence and a matched triple of 9s before the Source loses ten more cards"* — that the table must collectively satisfy from their Spellbooks.
- Demand intensity scales on **16+ values**: overtone-numbered demand cards are the crises, requiring combined contributions no single Spellbook can make — forced cooperation with real spellbook cost (components committed to the world are *bound*, not spent).
- Failure tightens the clock (banish from the Source — the same lever Spell Duels Tier 4 gives players, held by the game instead). Solo mode is the same system at one Spellbook with scaled demands.
- Endgame: a cooperative Trials variant — the table allocates *combined* power to the final Convergence trials; the group score is the campaign metric.

**Complexity vs. the pair:** medium — heavier rules overhead than Trials, lighter than Duels Tier 5; but its real cost is *balancing*, which is a different discipline (difficulty curves, not fairness).

**What Echo and 16+ specifically enable:** the opponent needs a voice that is **none of the players' colours** — demands expressed in the four base currents would double as information/denial about players' own suits, and couldn't be told apart from ordinary components in hand. A fifth, player-absent current is the clean NPC channel. And an escalating threat ladder needs numbers *above* what players can trivially hold — 16+ demand values read instantly as "beyond one wizard," which is the entire point.
- Neither asset is load-bearing in the base loop, so the co-op layer bolts on without touching it — exactly the modularity constraint.

**Why it's distinct and worth building:** it's the only direction aimed at a different *audience* rather than a different mechanism — solo players and co-op tables the current line can't serve at all (Duels needs an opponent; Trials needs rivals to out-score). Commercially it's the standard third leg of a modern line. Design-wise it recycles the game's existing tension (the depleting Source) as the villain, so the theme comes free.

## Side-by-side

| | **1 Fifth Current** | **2 Echoes of the Spent** | **3 High Mysteries** | **4 The Convergence** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Throughline owned | Wider substrate (5th suit, ladder, 6–7p) | The Drought as a third act | Vertical prestige race | Co-op / solo |
| Leans on | **Echo (fully)** | Echo (as spent magic) | **16+ (fully)**, Echo optional | Echo (as NPC voice) + 16+ (as threat scale) |
| Complexity vs. pair | Below both | ≈ Trials | Above Duels T4 — the big box | Medium; balancing-heavy |
| Cheapest to testable | ✔ (half-specified in STATE.md) | needs Drought-effect design | needs full condition/score design | needs difficulty-curve work |
| Overlap risk with the pair | None (substrate) | Low (Drought is unowned) | Low; small Trials touchpoint (new trial) | None (different mode) |
| Depends on | Base loop locked | Base + Drought pacing stable | Score table + Trials stable | Base loop locked |

**Sequencing observation (not a commitment):** Direction 1 is upstream of everything — if Echo ships as a full current, Directions 2 and 4 inherit a printed suit instead of introducing one, and Direction 3 gains its rarest claims. The line reads naturally as **1 → (2 or 4) → 3**, with 3 as the destination big-box the way Cities & Knights anchors Catan's line. But 1 is also the direction whose hidden rebalancing cost is largest — every score table and Trials pool moves — so "cheapest to testable" and "cheapest to finish" are not the same thing there.

**Naming dependency:** Direction consistency requires the Spell Duels rename in [[Expansion B - Spell Duels (Reconciled & Tiered)]] (Aether form "Echo" → **Imprint**) to hold — Echo must refer to exactly one thing across the line.
