# Stranger Evidence Plan (July 2026)

The entire live evidence base to date is Sam's circle, on a ruleset that predates every
v2.8 rule [FILE: product viability §1]. What friends tolerate — 90 minutes, verbal patching,
illegible cards, opaque scoring — is exactly what strangers won't. This plan generates the
project's first unbiased data and closes the two forks (F1, F2) that only a table can close.

---

## 1. Evidence principles — what to trust and distrust

**Trust:**
- **Behaviour over compliments.** What players *do* (ask to play again, reach for the clock,
  argue a line) outweighs anything said to Sam's face.
- **Unprompted description of the hook.** If a player spontaneously mentions the dying Source,
  the living melds, or the monument, the hook is landing. Record their exact words.
- **The replay *choice*** when a familiar alternative is genuinely on the table (§4).
- **Requests** — for interaction, for a rematch, to teach someone else — are honest signals.
- **Where laughter and groans land** — that maps the real emotional arc [FILE: product viability W5].

**Distrust:**
- **"I'd buy it" / "I liked it"** delivered to the designer — worthless unless paired with an
  actual replay choice.
- **Any feedback after "did you like it?"** — never ask that. Ask "walk me through your last turn."
- **Novelty preference** in one-night comparisons (new games win by default) — weight the *reasons*
  a player gives, not the vote [FILE: product viability E4].
- **Politeness-driven pretend-understanding** — probe comprehension by having players narrate a
  turn, not by asking "does that make sense?".
- **Replays chosen to be nice to Sam** — the cleaner signal comes when a friend hosts and Sam is absent.

**Standing rule for every session:** Sam does not explain design intent before or during play;
Sam may answer a rules question only by pointing at the written text.

## 2. Test sequence

Each test names the Decision Register item it unlocks.

### TS1 — Blind teach (= product viability E1 / design review T1)
- **Design question:** Can strangers start playing from written materials in ≤15 min with zero
  counter questions after the read? Is F1's failure *text* or *structure*?
- **Target players:** 3–4 strangers (café / BGG meetup), at least one rummy-literate.
- **Setup:** current rules + best-effort rewritten counter text (doubles as F1's control arm).
- **Sam may say:** nothing but "it's in the rules, here" (point at text).
- **Observe:** time-to-first-turn; questions per system; which rule is silently misplayed.
- **Record:** counter-questions per player in first 3 turns; time-to-first-correct-Recall; misplays.
- **Pass:** play starts <15 min, misplays self-correct, ≤0–1 counter questions after the read.
- **Fail:** teach stalls on counters again (≥2 players confused).
- **Decision unlocked:** **F1** (pass → text suffices/Option C; fail → structure, adopt gauge/round-trip).

### TS2 — Replay-choice (= E2) — the core conversion metric
- **Design question:** Does anyone *choose* game two when a familiar alternative is offered?
- **Target players:** the TS1 group, same sitting, time for two plays.
- **Setup:** after game one, offer Archmage again *or* a familiar alternative (§4).
- **Sam may say:** only the neutral script in §4.
- **Observe:** the choice; who advocates; what they'd "do differently" (= strategy space exists).
- **Record:** the choice; advocacy; verbatim "do differently" statements.
- **Pass:** ≥1 player argues for Archmage again, unprompted, with a build idea.
- **Fail:** unanimous switch to the familiar game.
- **Decision unlocked:** informs **F10** (is there a product here at all) and the hook's reality.

### TS3 — Visible Source clock A/B (= E6 / T9)
- **Design question:** Does making the Source count public change table talk and perceived tension?
- **Target players:** strangers; two short games or two tables.
- **Setup:** identical rules; arm B adds a face-up Source track. No other change.
- **Sam may say:** nothing.
- **Observe:** clock references per game; racing/stalling behaviour; "did the ending feel earned?".
- **Record:** clock-talk count per arm; any deliberate cast-throttling; one-line ending verdict.
- **Pass:** arm B produces visibly more clock-talk / racing.
- **Fail:** no behavioural difference between arms.
- **Decision unlocked:** **DR-CLOCK** (fail → visibility isn't enough; needs manipulability, a bigger call).

### TS4 — No-intent silent-observer (= E5)
- **Design question:** What game do strangers think they're playing? Clever or compliant (F-J)?
- **Target players:** a stranger table taught by a *player from a previous session* (also tests
  rules transmission), Sam silent or absent (friend reports back).
- **Setup:** current rules; mid-game the teacher asks each player "what are you trying to do right
  now?" [FILE: session 2's designed-but-never-asked probe — reuse it].
- **Sam may say:** nothing.
- **Observe:** answers naming a build/plan vs answers naming compliance ("waiting for counters");
  any spontaneous clock talk; where laughter/groans fall.
- **Record:** plan-answers vs compliance-answers per player; clock mentions; emotional-arc map.
- **Pass:** plans and clock-talk dominate.
- **Fail:** compliance answers dominate → F-J confirmed with strangers.
- **Decision unlocked:** validates/challenges **DR-HOOK** and **F1** at the experience level.

### TS5 — Comparison vs a familiar card/rummy game (= E4)
- **Design question:** What does Archmage give that rummy-family games don't — in players' words?
- **Target players:** 3–4 strangers who know rummy / Five Crowns-type games.
- **Setup:** one short familiar rummy-style game, then Archmage, same night. Debrief asks only
  "how were they different?" and "which would you bring to game night, and for whom?".
- **Sam may say:** only the two debrief questions.
- **Observe:** does anyone name the clock, living melds, or theme unprompted?
- **Record:** the differences named, verbatim; the "for whom" answers (feeds F10 audience).
- **Pass:** differences named match the protected hook.
- **Fail:** differences named are "longer", "more complicated", "more counters".
- **Decision unlocked:** informs **F10** (audience/band) and hook visibility.

**Passive throughout — TS6 unprompted-return (= E3):** leave the prototype available at a recurring
meetup; record whether anyone *asks* to play again within 2–3 weeks, and what they call it. One
unprompted request is a strong signal; silence confirms respectable-but-forgettable.

**Sequencing:** TS1+TS2 are one evening (the combined first session, §3). TS5 is a second evening.
TS3/TS4 ride later sessions. TS6 runs passively. None require Echo, new exports, or any print run.

## 3. Combined first session — design

The first stranger session must answer **both** the product-appeal question (does the hook land,
does anyone replay) **and** F1's text-vs-structure failure — in one evening.

- **Ruleset to use:** current v2.8 mechanics with the **rewritten control-arm counter text**. Run
  the control arm first (TS1). If comprehension fails in the first game, switch the second game to
  the **Capacity Gauge variant** (player mat + 3-phase turn, Recall deleted) — this makes game two
  double as the F1 A/B (TS/T4) *and* the replay (TS2). If comprehension passes, game two is a
  straight replay on the same rules.
- **Counters used:** control text in game one; Capacity Gauge in game two *only if* game one fails —
  do not pre-commit to the gauge. Do not build the round-trip redesign for this session (no written
  ruleset exists for it yet [GAP]).
- **What NOT to test yet:** Echo / 5–6p (run at 2–4p — the hook's best band); Ascension Trials;
  scoring-table edge values; card exports; anything requiring a print run.
- **Exact observations to collect:** time-to-first-turn; counter-questions per player (first 3 turns);
  time-to-first-correct-Recall; silent misplays; the replay choice (§4) + verbatim advocacy; the
  verbatim words used to describe the game; any spontaneous clock/monument mention; where groans/laughs land.
- **Result that justifies moving to Kiro implementation:** TS1 passes clean (≤1 counter question after
  the read, play <15 min) **and** ≥1 unprompted replay advocacy in TS2 → F1 closes toward Option C,
  and the hook has its first positive datum → Phase 3 (implementation of the validated direction) opens.
  If TS1 fails, F1 closes toward *structure* (gauge/round-trip) and implementation still waits on the
  gauge A/B result — but the session was not wasted: it produced the decisive F1 datum either way.

## 4. Replay-choice test — operational

- **Acceptable alternatives:** a short, familiar, comparable card game genuinely available at the
  table — e.g., a rummy variant, Five Crowns, Lost Cities, Fantasy Realms. It must be one the group
  would plausibly pick on its own merits, not a strawman.
- **Neutral ask (verbatim):** *"We've got time for one more — this again, or something else?"* Nothing
  more. No "did you like it?", no eye contact bias toward Archmage, no designer advocacy.
- **Genuine replay signal:** a player *argues for* Archmage unprompted, ideally naming something they
  want to try ("I want to go for an enchantment build" / "I'd race the clock harder"). A shrug-and-
  "sure" is not a signal. Politeness to Sam is not a signal — the cleanest version has a friend host
  and Sam absent.
- **Record the words:** whatever players call the game *is* the perceived hook — capture it verbatim.
  "The wizard rummy one" vs "the one where the world's dying" are opposite outcomes. Log every phrase
  used to describe or recommend it, attributed to player and moment.

## 5. Hook-visibility metric

Purpose: measure whether players perceive the hook's four objects, or only "rummy with counters."
Score each session on a simple perception ledger — did **any** player, **unprompted**, register:

- **The dying Source** — mentions the deck running down / time pressure / "how long left?".
- **The living Spellbook** — refers to their melds as an engine / something they're building, not
  as scored points.
- **The clock race** — deliberately throttles or accelerates casting; comments on ending timing.
- **The Drought** — reacts to the phase change as a climax (tension/urgency), not as admin.
- **"Rummy with counters"** — the negative marker: describes the game primarily as rummy + bookkeeping.

**Reading the ledger:** each of the first four registered unprompted by ≥1 player = the hook is
visible on that axis. The negative marker dominating (players describe counters/admin before any of
the four) = the design is hiding its best idea, regardless of how clean the rules are. **Target for
"proceed to build":** at least the Source and the Spellbook register unprompted, and the negative
marker does *not* dominate the replay-choice descriptions (§4). Until then, hook-visibility work
(DR-CLOCK, DR-DROUGHT, legible scoring) outranks mechanical streamlining [FILE: master plan §2 standing rule].

## 6. Anti-local-optimization guardrails for testing
- One question per session must be *killable* — design each session to end a Decision Register item,
  not to gather general impressions [FILE: design review §7].
- Any system iterated twice without new table evidence escalates to a redesign/cut decision, not a
  third tweak [FILE: product viability §9].
- Tag every design claim in the repo `friends-validated / stranger-validated / untested` and review
  it each session — most are currently `untested` wearing confident language [FILE: product viability §9].
