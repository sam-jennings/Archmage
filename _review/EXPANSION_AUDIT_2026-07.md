# Expansion Line Audit — 2026-07-16

Full audit of all eleven expansion concepts: stage, target audience, key contribution, unexplored areas, combination behaviour, and a potential score. Review-only; makes no decisions. Written to feed the expansions thread's open question: **which module gets the first expansion playtest slot.**

**"Potential" here means:** player value delivered × distinctness of the axis owned × commercial breadth, discounted by development cost, dependency on unstable base systems (v3.1 under test; F1 counter question open), and design risk. Score /10. Every concept has zero table contact except Trials — all scores are paper judgements.

## Stage ladder used

| Stage | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **S0 Sketch** | Hook + mechanical sketch, no full ruleset |
| **S1 Developed sketch** | Full ruleset drafted, minimal iteration |
| **S2 Shelf-ready v0.1** | Full rules, paper-iterated (bug log), fit analysis, untested |
| **S3 Playtest-ready** | S2 + session plan exists |
| **S4 Tested** | ≥1 live session |
| **S5 Locked** | Committed to the line |

# Part 1 — Module-by-module

## 1. Ascension Trials — S5 (locked) + deepest evidence base
- **Stage:** the only locked expansion. v2.8 rules in `rulebook/`, its own thread at candidate-shortlist stage (simulated 2p exact screening + seeded 3–6p S1 sims; C1/C4 candidates awaiting live tests). Reconciliation debt: F8 has 9 open propagation items; older 5–7p language stale.
- **Target:** experienced/strategic players who want the endgame to be a game — secret allocation, bluffing over builds. The "advanced variant" crowd.
- **Key contribution:** owns *how the game scores*. Turns evaluation from arithmetic into a hidden-information contest, and makes every mid-game build decision double as trial positioning.
- **Risk / gap:** math-heavy (power formula, six trials, tiebreakers) — the line's heaviest teach relative to the base game's lightness. 6p reward splits still unresolved.
- **Score: 8** — locked and furthest along; capped by complexity and by reconciliation debt that must be paid regardless.

## 2. Spell Duels — S2 (the most paper-iterated of the untested)
- **Stage:** reconciled & tiered (2026-07-02) from three source docs; 10 contradictions ruled, 7 open questions logged, five cumulative tiers built to overshoot at both ends. No playtests at any tier.
- **Target:** groups who want teeth — drama, bluffing, acting on a rival. Explicitly *not* the chill-social audience the base game currently lands with.
- **Key contribution:** owns *how players collide*. Frequency-clash chassis (higher card wins, defender wins ties), neighbour-only, never destroys builds, never touches capacity. The tier ladder is also a methodological asset: it finds the product weight at the table instead of guessing.
- **Risk / gap:** appetite unvalidated — Session 4's depth feedback asked for *strategy*, not *conflict*; nobody has said "I want to attack my neighbour." Tier 3's Transmute touches the learning economy (Q2) and must be re-checked against whatever counter design lands. 2p expected thin.
- **Score: 6.5** — excellent execution of a demand signal that doesn't exist yet. Test Tier 1 cheaply when an interaction-hungry table appears; don't lead with it.

## 3. The Schools — S3 (playtest template exists)
- **Stage:** v0.1 (2026-07-02), 9 bugs fixed on paper, session-plan template written (gated on the Stage-3 counter fix). With Last Rites, the most process-complete of the untested modules.
- **Target:** everyone — the classic second-purchase box. Replayability seekers; also the cheapest onboarding hook for lapsed playtesters ("same game, different wizard").
- **Key contribution:** owns *who you are*. Asymmetric start identity (talent + lean), one card per player, lightest teach in the line. Changes the first five minutes of every game rather than the last twenty.
- **Risk / gap:** balance across eight Schools is the entire (invisible) dev cost, and asymmetry multiplies every future module's checklist (already visible: Foci rule 5, Seals rule 5 both check against the Schools roster). Leans can't be finely tuned until the score table freezes (B9).
- **Score: 9** — highest appeal-to-cost ratio in the line, upstream of everything (identity makes every later box richer), and the only module that generates free testing mileage by making base-game retests attractive.

## 4. The Last Rites — S3 (playtest template exists)
- **Stage:** v0.1 (2026-07-02), 9 bugs fixed on paper, session-plan template written (same gate).
- **Target:** existing fans who feel the ending is flatter than the build-up — which per the product-viability audit is table-observed, not hypothetical. Planners; players who love a visible race.
- **Key contribution:** owns *the ending*. The Drought becomes a third act: public Rite row shapes play from turn one, endgame becomes a disassembly race (strip your engine vs. hold your patterns). Attacks the game's known weakest beat — the title moment.
- **Risk / gap:** hinges on the Drought being long enough to carry content (open question: base Droughts run short — if most games allow 1 Rite, the row is scenery). First-mover advantage at the Drought trigger accepted-but-flagged (B3).
- **Score: 8.5** — the only module whose demand signal comes from observed play ("the ending is quieter than the build-up"), independent of both open wounds (counters, score table), cheap to build.

## 5. Rituals & Mastery Seals — S2 (new 2026-07-16)
- **Stage:** v0.1, 8 bugs fixed on paper, fit analysis done (own module; sibling to Schools). 2 Rituals parked pending v3.1 wording checks.
- **Target:** the Session-4 depth-seeker directly — the Catan-refugee who found the base game pleasant but shallow. Achievement-oriented builders.
- **Key contribution:** owns *the mastery arc* — earned in-game identity. Public feats grant persistent rules lenses that live only while the structure stands; the internal/external Drought-persistence split is a lever nothing else uses. Only module deepening the **Learning phase**.
- **Risk / gap:** the central bet (holding structure is a real ongoing cost) is untested — if Spellbooks rarely restructure, Seals become claim-once-keep-forever and the module deflates (open question 1). Quiet by nature; may read as "eight small buffs" to the very audience it targets.
- **Score: 7.5** — direct answer to the depth brief with near-zero component cost, but its tension depends on a base-game behaviour (restructuring frequency) that v3.1's Reshape changes are actively moving.

## 6. Foci & Conduits — S2 (new 2026-07-16, revised same day)
- **Stage:** v0.1, 12 bugs fixed on paper (incl. Sam's Deep Source/Essence redirect), fit analysis done (own module; closest sibling Rituals).
- **Target:** the engine-builder crowd — the largest hobby segment adjacent to this game (Wingspan/TfM players). Also the Session-4 depth-seeker, from the "unique parts and combos" side rather than the "earned feats" side.
- **Key contribution:** owns *the engine arc* — the four missing EB signatures at once: piece differentiation (unique Foci), a draft arc (exclusive public row), chaining (Conduits — the line's first spell-to-spell interaction), escalation (wide/tall-engine rewards). Essence layer makes clock contact a player choice (burn vs. refine — DR-HOOK amplified). Only module deepening the **Casting phase**.
- **Risk / gap:** the most economy-adjacent module (Essence is a new currency; forge costs and the 5+ tap gate are three untested numbers). Runaway-engine is the genre risk. Heaviest teach of the three new modules.
- **Score: 8** — the strongest *commercial* answer to the depth brief and the most differentiated module in the untested stack; discounted for economy contact while v3.1 is mid-test and for numeric tuning surface.

## 7. The Null Cabal — S2 (new 2026-07-16)
- **Stage:** v0.1, 9 bugs fixed on paper, incl. the fully-specified 3p model. Fit analysis done (own module; Trials the recommended pairing).
- **Target:** social-deduction groups of 4–6; game nights, not couples (no 2p, by design). A genuinely *different buyer* from every other box.
- **Key contribution:** owns *trust and interpretation* — the one axis nothing else touches. Arithmetic deduction (Veil sums, not vibes) is a real innovation over behavioural-read traitor games and is what makes 3p viable.
- **Risk / gap:** mood mismatch — the base game's observed identity is chill and social; a traitor changes the emotional register of the whole evening. All-or-nothing Cabalist may be dead weight late (B4). Whether arithmetic deduction converges at better-than-chance is the whole bet.
- **Score: 6.5** — distinctive and well-built, but it serves the audience the game *could* have rather than the one it demonstrably has. Right box, probably later in the line.

## 8. The Overtones — S0 (sketch, Direction 1)
- **Stage:** hook + mechanical sketch in the Post-Echo doc. No ruleset.
- **Target:** enfranchised fans — the big-box destination purchase. Aspirational players; the strongest table-presence pitch ("what do those big numbers do?").
- **Key contribution:** owns *vertical prestige* and spends the line's one reserved design-space asset (values 16–20). The only module that is aspirational *during* play — a public mid-game arc neither Trials (retrospective) nor Duels (horizontal) provides.
- **Risk / gap:** built out of the score table — cannot be developed until scoring stabilises (v3.1 is mid-confirm/kill; the QUEUE's three-decision scoring cluster is open). Heaviest direction (≥ Duels T4).
- **Score: 7** — high ceiling as flagship, hard-blocked today. Correctly parked; its 16+/band/exclusive-row space is already being defended by every new module's design rules, which is the cheap way to keep it viable.

## 9. The Convergence — S0 (sketch, Direction 4)
- **Stage:** hook + mechanical sketch. No ruleset. Salvage notes attach to it (World Stability as a competitive-table mode; Round definition + Source-depth markers as base prerequisites).
- **Target:** co-op tables, families, and solo players — the only box aimed at audiences the line structurally cannot serve today. Solo support is a hard market expectation for a modern release.
- **Key contribution:** owns *mode of play*. The dying Source as the villain is thematically free; demands written in the game's own pattern language.
- **Risk / gap:** difficulty-curve tuning across counts 1–6 is a different discipline and ~10× the tuning sessions; sensible only after the production pipeline exists. Also the intended second home for the Cabal (co-op traitor), so its design carries two modules' futures.
- **Score: 7** — biggest audience expansion in the portfolio; the discount is entirely sequencing and cost, not concept.

## 10. The Living Array (Concept A) — S1 (developed sketch, partially stale)
- **Stage:** full v2 ruleset in the player-interaction doc (June 2026) — but written against 2–7p and pre-dates the product model and the standing governance rules; needs a light reconciliation pass before any use.
- **Target:** any group wanting more interaction without more weight. Honestly: closer to a base-game variant than a sellable box (zero components).
- **Key contribution:** converts the Array from vending machine to contested market with one rule. Its real value is *instrumental* — the cheapest possible test of "does added interaction improve this game at all," and its take-rule has already been borrowed by the 2p Rival's Gambit package (see `_review/2P_STRATEGIC_DEPTH_DEEP_DIVE_2026-07.md`).
- **Risk / gap:** as a product it's too thin to charge for; Transfiguration contention risk at low counts.
- **Score: 4 as a product / 8 as an instrument** — recommend formally reclassifying it as a free variant / test protocol rather than a line entry, and folding its current life into the 2p contention work where it's already being used.

## 11. The Conclave (Concept C) — S1 (developed sketch, partially stale)
- **Stage:** full v1 ruleset (pacts, influence, edicts, hidden agendas, 2p Cold Pact reduction) in the player-interaction doc; same 2–7p staleness; never revisited since June.
- **Target:** negotiation-loving social groups, 3+. The "big box for the invested table."
- **Key contribution:** owns *politics* — brokered power, enforced pacts, secret simultaneous Edict votes (deliberately reusing the Trials allocation DNA).
- **Risk / gap:** hardest genre to get right (kingmaking, collusion, table-talk downtime — all acknowledged in-doc). Overlaps the Cabal on hidden-agenda space: the standing ruling (Cabal replaces the Conclave's agenda deck, never both) already constrains it. Farthest from the game's current identity.
- **Score: 5** — a destination, not a plan. Fine on the shelf; nothing about it should be developed before the line has an audience.

# Part 2 — Unexplored areas

**Owned but empty (sketches holding an axis with no ruleset):**
- **Solo/co-op** (Convergence) — the largest unserved audience; nothing playable exists.
- **16+ prestige space** (Overtones) — reserved and defended by every module's design rules, but undeveloped.

**Genuinely unowned axes — no concept exists:**
- **Dedicated 2p depth.** Multiple modules are weakest at 2 (Duels thin, Foci row uncontested, Cabal excluded, Conclave reduced) and the base 2p contention problem is being solved *base-side* (Rival's Gambit). No expansion targets couples — arguably the most common real-world count. Worth an explicit decision: is 2p depth base-game territory (current de facto answer) or a box?
- **Team play (2v2).** Nothing. Veils/Pacts machinery could carry it cheaply someday.
- **Campaign / narrative progression across games.** Nothing carries state between sessions. Seals ("what you have become") is the natural seed if ever wanted.
- **Scenario / variable-setup content.** No module varies the starting world (Source composition, seeded Arrays, asymmetric setups). Cheapest possible content lever; currently unused.
- **"More of the same" content.** No box adds new spell patterns, new pattern *types*, or deck variety without new systems — the standard first-expansion move in the genre, absent here. (Closest: Overtones extends pattern space upward.)
- **Downward complexity.** The whole line points up. No family/simplified direction exists. Probably correct — but it's a choice being made by omission.

**Explored and deliberately closed (don't reopen without new evidence):**
- Randomness/events (Arcane Weather) — dropped, conflicts with the sequencing-skill spine.
- Spellbook-attacking offense (Disjunction/Dissonance) — superseded by Duels' never-destroy spine.
- Fifth-suit-as-expansion — realized as the Echo extension; spent.

# Part 3 — Combinations

Governance (EXPANSION_INDEX): complexity modules define possibilities, dynamic modules define incentives; caps scale down under social load; hidden info never modifies math; effect-template discipline. These four rules are already doing real work — every 2026-07-16 module cites them.

## Natural bundles (how a line could ship)

1. **Identity bundle:** Schools + Rituals & Seals — dealt + earned identity, checked line-by-line against each other already. Strongest thematic pairing.
2. **Depth/engine bundle:** Foci & Conduits + Rituals & Seals — Casting + Learning bookends; jointly the full answer to the Session-4 brief. Physical watch item: a spell carrying chit + card + host.
3. **Endgame bundle:** Trials + Last Rites — Rites reshape what Trials evaluate; riders already written.
4. **Social bundle:** Null Cabal + Trials (dedicated agendas), everything else capped down beneath it.
5. **Flagship (later):** Overtones + Schools (asymmetric routes up the rack) + Rites (overtone-kicker sacrifice agony) — three pre-ruled contact points, all good.

## Line-level frictions to keep watching

- **Grammar crowding:** three modules now use "public row visible from turn one" (Rites, Rituals, Foci) and two use "build structure → gain ongoing ability" (Seals, Foci). Each is differentiated on paper (spend/hold/race; lens/part), but stacked at one table they may *feel* like one mechanism. This is the strongest argument for the bundles above rather than free mixing.
- **The modifier-stacking rule** (max one modifier per effect instance, owner picks — Foci rule 6, from the Inner Flame precedent) is now line law across Schools/Seals/Foci. It belongs in EXPANSION_INDEX next to the four governance rules, not buried in two module docs. *(Done alongside this audit — now governance rule 5 in EXPANSION_INDEX.)*
- **Cabal is the universal downscaler** — every persistent-effect module halves under it. Correct per governance, but it means the social box and the depth boxes actively dilute each other: they target different evenings, and marketing should say so.
- **Duels Tiers 3+ conflict with almost everything** (economy contact Q2, suspicion load under Cabal, Bend-vs-clock alongside Rites' Long Dusk). Tiers 1–2 are the combinable product; 3–5 are a variant ceiling.

## Implication for the first expansion playtest slot (options, not a decision)

- **Schools** — lowest cost, but note the contamination risk: it should *not* ride along on the v3.1 confirm/kill session (that test needs a clean read). First *dedicated* slot or first post-v3.1 casual session.
- **Rituals & Seals / Foci & Conduits** — both answer the depth brief; both have measures that read cleaner *after* v3.1 lands (Reshape behaviour; Essence-vs-clock). Testing either mid-experiment confounds two variables.
- **Last Rites** — independent of v3.1's contested systems; a legitimate "test something while the economy settles" pick, and it probes the Drought-length question the base game needs answered anyway.

The sequencing pressure all points the same way: **v3.1 confirm/kill first, clean; then the first expansion slot.** Within that, Schools (cheapest signal) or Last Rites (most independent signal) lead on method; Foci & Conduits leads on the depth brief if Sam wants the Session-4 answer tested soonest.
