## Recommendation (Q2)

At 2p, an **unbounded Reshape adds +40.1** points over the as-played book on average and lands on a Transfiguration in **85%** of games — the exploit reproduces. The question is which lever removes it.


**Size cap works.** Capping a single Reshape by spell size collapses the spike (cap 6 → +0.1, cap 8 → +0.8, cap 10 → +5.7); a cap of ~6 removes ≥80% of it **without touching the score table**. This is the primary fix.

**Cost-scaling by counters is ineffective at the tested rate** (4 cards/counter): it only trims the spike to +35.3, because players hold **5.5 counters** on average at game end — enough to pay for a large Reshape anyway. Cost-scaling would need a much tighter rate to bite.

Independently, **Part A shows a genuine pricing inversion**: a 7-card Transfiguration is easier to reach than a 5-card Perfect Transmutation yet scores 5 more, and Transfiguration's price/implied ratio climbs with size while PT's stays near 1. A bounded Reshape does not fix this, so the smallest complementary table change is to flatten the Transfiguration column relative to Perfect Transmutation.


**Bottom line:** bound the end-game Reshape (size cap is the clean, table-free lever); optionally add a small Transfiguration re-price to correct the standalone inversion. Confirm against the tables above and log any adopted change under `meta/decisions/`.

## Model limitations

- Greedy, identical, non-opponent-aware agents; no contest for the Array. A 2p exploit may be partly regulated by blocking at higher counts (Session 4 R2) — Part B measures the pool, not live contention.

- Hand-model reach-probabilities are chase ceilings, not in-play attainment.

- The Reshape evaluator forges the single best spell from the pool (vs keeping the committed book); multi-spell pool partitioning is a documented v2 refinement.

- Conjuration card values are captured exactly (rig `vals` field), so the pool reconstruction is exact for hand + enchantment + conjuration cards.
