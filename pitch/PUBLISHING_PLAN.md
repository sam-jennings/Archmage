# From finished design to retail shelf — the plan

**The route in one paragraph.** You are licensing the game to a publisher. They pay you a royalty (typically 4–6% of wholesale, often with a €3k–10k advance) and take over art, development, manufacturing, marketing, and distribution. Your job is only: build a pitch kit, put the game in front of the right editors, survive their evaluation, and sign a sane contract. Industry-normal timeline from first pitch to a shelf copy is **2–3 years** — most of it publisher-side.

**What you're selling, in publisher terms:** *Arcane Drought* — competitive card game, 2–4 players (5–6 with the Echo deck), ~30–60 min, ages ~10+, fantasy/wizard theme, rummy-meld engine hybrid with a shared depleting resource. Small component set (62–79 cards + counters + reference cards) → low production cost → low risk for a publisher. That profile is the single biggest thing in your favour: cheap-to-make card games with one clear hook are exactly what mid-size European publishers sign from unknown designers.

## Step 0 — Build the pitch kit (now → end of Aug 2026, ~4–6 weeks part-time)

Nothing here changes the design; it packages it.

1. **Sell sheet, final pass.** One page. You already have `pitch/archmage_pitch_sheet.html` — finish it to the Lesson 13 standard: hook in the first sentence, the Big Three as ranges (2–6 players / 10+ / 30–60 min), exact component list ("62-card core deck, 17-card Echo module, ~40 counters…"), who-you-are / what-you-do / how-you-win, one strong mood image (no commissioned art). Use the final title **Arcane Drought**.
2. **Pitch video, ~3 minutes.** Phone camera over the table: hook (30s), a turn (90s), why it ends with drama (30s). UKGE and several publishers ask for exactly this ([UKGE pitch-video guidance](https://www.ukgamesexpo.co.uk/whats-on/publisherdesigner/speed-dating/pitch-video/)).
3. **English rules, standalone.** Publishers evaluate from the rulebook without you in the room. One editing pass of the current rulebook for a stranger-readable v1.
4. **Two–three clean prototype copies** via printenbind.nl (specs already on file). Functional beats pretty — publishers say so explicitly ([Deep Print](https://www.deep-print-games.com/en/dein-spiel-bei-uns/)).
5. **Evidence file.** Publishers ask "how much has this been tested, and by strangers?" One paragraph of honest numbers: sessions, player counts, blind tests. (999 Games requires 20+ tests including outsiders as a submission condition.)
6. **Pitch tracker.** A simple table: publisher / date sent / material / reply / follow-up date. You will run 20+ conversations; memory won't cut it.
7. **Subscribe to the [Cardboard Edison Compendium](https://cardboardedison.com/directoryinfo)** (~$2/month, 350+ publishers with live submission status). Optional: [Pam Walls' monthly open-submissions list](https://pamwallsgamedesign.substack.com/).

> Honesty note: publishers' #1 stated rejection reason for new designers is "pitched too early." The plan assumes your premise (design final). If the v3.1 confirm/kill and any stranger-blind evidence aren't done when Step 1 starts, do them first — that's evidence-gathering, not redesign.

## Step 2 — Autumn events on your doorstep (Oct–Nov 2026)

This is where being in Amsterdam pays off (see the location section).

1. **SPIEL Essen, 22–25 Oct 2026** ([site](https://www.spiel-essen.de/en/)) — the world's biggest board game fair, ~2.5h from Amsterdam. Publishers take 15–30 min pitch meetings at their booths, **booked in advance**: email your Tier 1/2 targets in **August–September** asking for an Essen appointment, sell sheet attached. Go for two days; bring a prototype and 20 printed sell sheets. Walking up without an appointment sometimes works at smaller booths — have the 60-second verbal pitch drilled.
2. **Spellenspektakel, 7–8 Nov 2026, Jaarbeurs Utrecht** ([site](https://spellenspektakel.nl/?lang=en)) — the Dutch national fair; every Benelux publisher attends. Ideal for 999 Games / White Goblin / Quined conversations started in Step 1.
3. **Monthly, from now: Spellenmaakgilde playtest nights** ([guild](https://spellenmaakgilde.nl/), [Meetup](https://www.meetup.com/nl-nl/prototype-testing-meetup/)) — the Dutch designers' guild; the Amsterdam chapter (ABGD) meets at OBA Oosterdok. Not for finding a publisher directly — for drilling your pitch and teach on strangers monthly, and because Dutch publisher scouts know this scene.

## Step 3 — Winter follow-ups and the two big 2027 applications (Dec 2026 – Apr 2027)

1. Work the tracker: follow-ups, prototype requests (a publisher evaluating your prototype typically sits on it **2–6 months** — normal, chase gently quarterly).
2. **Apply to UK Games Expo Publisher–Designer Speed Dating** (Birmingham, late May/early June 2027). Applications open early in the year; selection is from your **sell sheet + rules sheet + optional pitch video**, and only ~24–36 of 100+ applicants get in — your Step 0 kit is the application. Two 90-minute sessions, a different publisher every 5 minutes. [Details](https://www.ukgamesexpo.co.uk/whats-on/publisherdesigner/speed-dating/).
3. **Register for the Göttingen Game Designers' Convention (June 2027)** — Europe's dedicated designer-publisher matchmaking event: ~80 editors from ~34 publishers walking the hall specifically to find games ([SAZ event page](https://www.spieleautorenzunft.de/eventleser/spieleautor-innen-treffen-in-goettingen-2026-game-designers-convention-in-goettingen-2026.html)). **The 152 author tables sold out within hours in 2026** — join [SAZ](https://www.spieleautorenzunft.de/) (the designers' guild; also gives you model contracts and contract-checking) and book the moment registration opens, likely ~March 2027.
4. Second submission wave from the Cardboard Edison Compendium — pick another 5–8 whose catalogs contain a 30–60 min card game like yours.

## Step 5 — The offer and the contract (whenever it lands; plan for mid-2027 → mid-2028)

Realistic expectation: a good first game signs after **20–40 pitches over 6–24 months**. One "yes" is all you need.

When an offer comes:

- **Standard terms:** 4–6% royalty of *wholesale* (check the calculation base — same % on different bases differs hugely), advance €3k–10k against royalties (you keep it regardless), publisher owns art/title/development decisions.
- **Non-negotiables to insist on:** a **publication deadline (max 3 years) with full rights reversion** if they don't publish; royalty statements at least twice yearly; your designer credit on the box. Ask for consultation rights on development changes (you rarely get veto).
- **Get the contract checked.** SAZ membership includes contract advice and model contracts; the TTGDA publishes examples too. Budget one hour with an IP-literate lawyer if anything smells off.
- Expect them to possibly retheme, retitle, or trim the 5–6p module. That's the deal you chose by not self-publishing — the hook is what you protect in conversation, and yours is strong enough to survive development.

## Step 6 — After signing (their clock, not yours)

Publisher development 6–12 months (you respond to questions, playtest their changes), then production and shipping 6–12 months, then release — usually timed to Essen or spring. Your only jobs: be responsive, be pleasant to work with, and start designing your next game — a second design in your bag doubles every future pitch meeting.

## Does Amsterdam matter? Yes — in your favour

- You live ~2.5h from **Essen** (world's biggest fair), ~4h from **Göttingen** (best designer-publisher matchmaking event in Europe), 1h flight from **UKGE**, and 30 min from **Spellenspektakel/Ducosim**. Most designers on earth would trade locations with you.
- The **Benelux publisher cluster** (999 Games, White Goblin, Quined, Jumping Turtle, plus Belgian houses) is local — same-language relationships, cheap prototype shipping, in-person meetings.
- A **monthly designers' guild playtest night runs in your city** (Spellenmaakgilde/ABGD at OBA Oosterdok).
- The industry's working language is English; German publishers evaluate English rules and prototypes as standard. No barrier.
- EU postage means sending prototypes to any European publisher is days and euros, not customs forms.

## Money out of pocket (estimates)

Cardboard Edison ~€25/yr · SAZ membership ~€100/yr · 3 prototypes ~€150–250 · Essen ticket+travel ~€150 · Spellenspektakel ~€40 · Göttingen table+trip ~€200–300 · UKGE trip ~€350–450. **Total ≈ €1,000–1,500 over 12 months.** No other spending is required — anyone asking for more is selling you something.
