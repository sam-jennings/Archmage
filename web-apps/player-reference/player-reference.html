The goal

Arrange components into spells in your Spellbook, drawing on the Source while it lasts. When the Source empties, the Drought begins — and when it ends, the most refined Spellbook wins on Recognition Points.

Set-up
  • 2–4 players: four energies × ranks 1–15 (60 cards + 2 wilds = 62 cards).
  • 5–6 players: five energies incl. Echo × ranks 1–15 (75 cards + 4 wilds = 79 cards).
  • Deal 7 cards to each player.
  • Place 5 face-up to form the Array. Place the rest face-down as the Source.
  • Each player discards 1 card to the Arcane Reserve (the Opening).
  • Each player takes their starting counters — 1 (2–4 players) or 2 (5–6 players). Choose a random starting player.

The Drought has begun

The instant the Source runs dry, shuffle the Array and the Arcane Reserve together into the Released Reserve. When the Released Reserve is fully depleted, the contest ends immediately — evaluate your Spellbooks.

Learning actions — 1 counter each

Learn

Place a new spell from your hand into your Spellbook.

3-card Enchantment: +1 counter (from your next turn). 4-card: +3 counters. 5-card: +5 counters.

Empower

Add components from your hand to an existing spell; may convert among Conjuration, Transfiguration, or Perfect Transmutation — but not into or out of an Enchantment.

Reshape

Break down and rebuild one or more spells using the same components. One counter per spell broken down.

Breaking a 3-card Enchantment: −1 counter. A 4-card: −3 counters. A 5-card: −5 counters. Stepping down one size loses the ladder difference (5→4 −2, 4→3 −2, 3→dissolve −1).

Unlearn

Return one or more components from a single spell; the remainder stays valid or the spell dissolves. Components can't be learned again until next turn. For runs, remove only an end component.

1 counter regardless of how many components you return. Same Enchantment warnings apply.

Counters & capacity

How many spells you cast and actions you take.

Counters are your capacity, not a spent resource. They work twice each turn: once to cast, then — after the Recall — once to learn.

  • Start with 1 counter (2–4 players) or 2 (5–6 players).
  • Each 3-card Enchantment learned: +1 counter; a 4-card: +3 counters, permanently.
  • A 5-card Enchantment: +5 counters (5–6 player games).
  • Capacity you gain applies from your next turn; capacity you lose applies immediately — you can't reduce or dissolve an Enchantment unless you can pay the action and absorb the loss now.
  • Break one down and the bonus is lost immediately, proportional to the ladder step.

Recognition Points

When the Released Reserve is fully depleted, the contest ends immediately. Tally Recognition Points from every spell in your Spellbook — highest total wins.

Tiebreaker: most spells → most components in largest single spell.

Reading the table
  • Perfect Transmutations score highest per component.
  • A 4-card Enchantment spikes to 10 points; a 5-card reaches 16 (5–6 player games).
  • One long spell usually beats two short ones.
