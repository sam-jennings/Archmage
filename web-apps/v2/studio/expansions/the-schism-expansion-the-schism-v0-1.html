The Schism — v0.1 Rules

Two claimants. One dying Source. One severs the flow — the other chooses.

A dedicated 2-player expansion that replaces the Collection phase with a cut-and-choose market: each round the failing Source tears open into a Rift of 4 revealed components — one is tithed to the Arcane Reserve, the player without the Severance severs the remaining 3 into two shares, and the Severance holder claims one. Shares are always unequal, so every round whoever takes fewer components takes (or keeps) the Severance: the choice seat and the initiative. Two cumulative tiers (the Severing is a complete module; Rift cards add variety). 2p only, by design — the line's first count-gated box.

Status: shelf-ready on paper, untested. Written to the 2p-dedicated-depth brief (audit 2026-07-16: the one genuinely unowned axis; multiple modules weakest at 2). Boundary decision (Sam, 2026-07-17, meta/decisions/2026-07-17-2p-depth-box-the-schism.md): 2p health stays base-side and free (the Rival's Gambit contention layer); 2p depth is this box, built on top. Same-day revisions (Sam's review): the Tithe added to fix Drought starvation (B11); open-information identity adopted, veiled-core fix rejected (B12); Severance coupled to initiative (B13); calibration flipped to reveal-4/sever-3 after the clock comparison (B14).

Why this module exists — the 2p brief

The deep dive (_review/2P_STRATEGIC_DEPTH_DEEP_DIVE_2026-07.md) found the structural root: the base game's interaction is all emergent from crowding, so at 2p it evaporates — and great dedicated 2p games don't scale a multiplayer market down, they design the head-to-head market as its own object (7 Wonders Duel, Targi, Jaipur). The Schism is that object. Against the four interaction channels:

  • Contested resources: every card of income now arrives through a cut both players shaped. Nothing is free and safe; the Session-4 "parallel puzzles" failure mode is structurally impossible.

  • Information: the Schism is deliberately an open-information duel (Sam, 2026-07-17). Rift income is public and hands converge toward known; the depth is the deep dive's escape #1 — make the read actionable — taken all the way: you sever against everything you can see of your rival's plan, and they choose knowing you did. Escape #2 (re-hiding information) is explicitly not this module's business; it stays Veiled Power's territory.

  • The Source clock: the Rift burns the Source in visible, countable chunks — the dying world is torn open on the table every round. DR-HOOK amplified, not diluted.

  • Endgame: the Tithe feeds a roughly doubled Released Reserve that both players watched being curated, and the Last Schism (below) drafts it head-to-head — the ending grows from ~29% of the game's rounds (base 2p) to ~40%, and it's contested instead of parallel top-decking.

Deliberately not delivered: teeth during the Casting phase (Spell Duels' territory) and endgame evaluation change (Trials/Veiled Power, base-side opt-in per 2026-07-09-2p-trials-not-default.md).

Fit: why its own module

  • Not a fold-in to the Living Array — the take-rule (base-side, Rival's Gambit) makes Collection takes denial within the existing market grammar; the Schism replaces the grammar. It also answers the audit's Living Array note: the 2p contention role the Array variant was moonlighting in now has a real home.

  • Not Spell Duels — Duels is collision during casting (frequency clash); this is market structure. No rules contact; Duels T1 could stack on top later if a 2p table wants teeth mid-turn.

  • Not Foci — different draft grammar family: Foci is an exclusive public row raced over many turns; the Rift is a flow, cut and consumed each round. Noted for the line's grammar-crowding watch all the same.

  • Not base rules — the boundary decision: the base 2p game must be healthy without purchase (Rival's Gambit levers stay free); the box sells depth, not the fix.

Throughline owned: the head-to-head market — who gets what, who cedes what, who chooses next. First module in the line built for exactly one count.

Design rules (binding on every Rift card, present and future)

  1. 2p only. The Schism never appears at 3+, and no other module may reference it.

  2. Never touches counters, capacity, casting/learning action counts, or RP (the protected economy; the Rift never scores).

  3. Every card entering play through a Rift comes from the Source (or the Array, itself refilled from the Source). No new zones, no card destruction — the Rift redistributes what base play would have drawn anyway, splitting it between the two hands and the Arcane Reserve; only who gets what and rate (bounded 2–5 revealed/round) change.

  4. Rift-card effects vary only: Rift size, tithe count, cut constraints, information (preview / face-down), and bounded Array contact (effect-template discipline, governance rule 4).

  5. Open-information identity (Sam, 2026-07-17): Rift income is public; the module never re-hides what play has revealed. Symmetric gambles (a face-down Rift component, blind to both players) are permitted; effects that force hand reveals are banned (feel-bad, and redundant when income is already open). Hidden information, where it exists, changes interpretation and timing only, never costs or values (governance rule 3).

  6. Suit-agnostic wording — nothing names an energy. (Echo never appears in 2p decks, so the standing 2–4p/5–6p compatibility rule is satisfied vacuously; flagged in the index as the first count-gated module.)

  7. The Drought stays the ending. The Tithe feeds the Released Reserve during normal play only; the Last Schism redistributes it, never adds to it or removes from it.

Tier 1 — The Severing

Components

  • 1 Severance token.

  • 1 Rift mat (share zones + a tithe space; optional but keeps cuts legible).

Setup

As base 2p (including whichever Rival's Gambit base-side levers are in use — see Contact, below). Give the Severance token to a random player — the cost of not holding it is priced by the market from Rift 1.

The Rift (replaces the Collection phase)

At the start of each round:

  1. Reveal 4 components from the Source — the Rift.

  2. The player without the Severance — the severer — sets 1 revealed component into the Arcane Reserve: the Tithe. (Neither of you gets it — yet. It returns in the Drought, contested.)

  3. The severer splits the remaining 3 into two shares, any way (2/1 or 3/0 — empty shares are legal).

  4. The Severance holder claims one share into hand; the severer takes the other.

  5. The Severance moves to whoever took fewer components. Shares of 3 are always unequal, so the token is contested every round — the holder keeps it only by taking less. (Equal shares, possible under some Rift cards, leave it where it is.)

  6. The Severance holder takes the first turn of the round. Turn order within a round is fixed; between rounds it follows the token.

Both players then skip the Collection phase entirely: turns begin at Casting. Cast draws (Conjuration/PT) and Array exchanges (Transfiguration/PT) work exactly as in the base game.

Three cards to the players each round, one to the Drought's pool. Every cut is live: which card to tithe, how to split, and the standing bill — claim the bigger share and hand over the upper hand, or take less and keep it. Income against control is the whole economy, and the severer sets the price.

The Array under the Schism

The Array keeps its base-side 2p size and serves cast exchanges only — with Collection takes gone, the Rival's Gambit take-rule and safety valve are dormant while the Schism is in play (they govern a phase that no longer exists). The same goes for the proposed Array Attunement gate (2026-07-17-array-attunement-tf-gate, base-side): it conditions Collection-phase takes, so it is vacuous under the box. Contention has moved from the Array to the cut.

The Last Schism (the Drought)

The severing continues over the Released Reserve — now roughly doubled by the game's Tithes — with no Tithe and the same cut: reveal 3 at the top of each Drought round (or all remaining, if fewer), sever (2/1 or 3/0), claim; players skip the Drought Collection draw; the token keeps moving by the same rule and its holder keeps leading the round. When the Released Reserve is empty, the contest ends as normal.

One grammar all game: the Drought simply has nothing left to tithe. The pool is deep enough for a real third act (~40% of the game's rounds vs ~29% in base 2p), and the last components both players need arrive through a cut a rival shaped.

Timing edges

  • If revealing the Rift empties the Source, complete the Rift fully, then the Drought begins (consistent with the proposed source-emptying-timing rule: finish the atomic action).

  • Partial Rifts (either pool running out): a 3-card Rift tithes 1 and severs 2 (1/1 or 2/0); a 2-card Rift tithes 1 and severs the last card 1/0; a 1-card Rift skips the Tithe and severs 1/0. In the Drought (no Tithe), sever whatever is revealed by the same rule. Same procedure, every size.

  • If cast draws empty the Source mid-turn, the Drought begins per base rules; the Last Schism starts from the next round.

Tier 2 — Rift cards (add after Tier 1 tests clean)

Components

  • 16 Rift cards (8 designs × 2). Shuffle at setup; flip the top card at the start of each round, before revealing the Rift. It shapes this Rift only. Reshuffle when exhausted. Not used during the Drought (the Last Schism stays clean).

The eight designs

Rift cardThis Rift...
Deep Riftreveals 5: tithe 1, sever 4 (2/2 is legal — on equal shares the token stays put).
Dry Riftreveals 3: tithe 1, sever 2 (1/1 or 2/0) — the brake round.
Veiled Riftreveals 3 face-up + 1 face-down (blind to both); it may be tithed unseen or placed in a share unseen — revealed only when claimed.
Forked Riftafter the Tithe, the severer adds 1 card from the Array to the split (sever 4; refill the Array after the Rift resolves).
Mirrored Riftbefore severing, reveal the top card of the Source to both players (it stays — next round's preview).
Hungry Riftno empty shares — the split must be 2/1.
Still Riftdoesn't open: no Rift, no Tithe this round; each player draws 1 from the Source on their own turn (the base Collection, back for one round).
Torn Riftdouble Tithe — tithe 2, sever 2 (1/1 or 2/0); the Drought swallows the round.

Why this isn't Arcane Weather (dropped: randomness/events conflict with the sequencing-skill spine — don't reopen without new evidence): Rift cards are revealed before any decision in the round, are symmetric information, and touch only the market's shape — never spell resolution, never mid-loop. Players plan around them; nothing disrupts a plan already in motion. It is still the module's riskiest element and the first thing to cut if it tests as noise rather than texture (kill criterion in the open questions). The Veiled Rift is additionally on watch under the open-information identity (B12) — first individual card to swap if it plays against the module's chess-like read.

Iteration log — bugs found and fixed before v0.1

  • B1 — Dual market layers. The first draft ran the Rift alongside base Collection and the Rival's Gambit take-rule — two market grammars per round, redundant contention, doubled income. Fix: the Rift replaces Collection; the Array becomes cast-exchange-only; the take-rule goes dormant under the box (precedence stated, not implied).

  • B2 — Equal shares undefined. An equal cut (2/2 under the Deep Rift; 1/1 under some Rift cards) left the Severance rider with no smaller share. Fix: on equal shares the token stays put. Under the default sever-3, shares are always unequal — the token is contested every round by construction.

  • B3 — The empty-share gambit audited. Instinct said ban empty shares; analysis says keep them: offering 3/0 is a real bid (all the income to your rival, the choice seat contested), and claiming the empty share is a real counter-bid (zero income, keep control). Income-vs-tempo, self-balancing, severer-priced. Kept; Hungry Rift varies it.

  • B4 — Clock contact accepted, then repriced. The pre-Tithe draft's reveal-4 (all to players) was cut to 3 for burn reasons; with the Tithe the arithmetic changed and the comparison was rerun — see B14 for where the calibration landed. Dry and Still Rifts remain brakes inside the Tier-2 deck. Target unchanged: 30–60 min at 2p.

  • B5 — Odd remainders unified. Partial final Rifts needed no special case: every size severs by the same rule (see Timing edges). No extra rules text.

  • B6 — Array-rider ambiguity. Forked Rift originally refilled the Array mid-Rift, changing what later steps saw. Fix: the Array refills only after the Rift fully resolves.

  • B7 — Face-down favoured the severer. The Veiled Rift first let the severer peek before placing — information asymmetry that breaks the cut logic and governance rule 3. Fix: blind to both; the gamble is symmetric.

  • B8 — Hand-reveal Rift card cut. A "both players reveal 2 hand cards" design was cut — forced hand exposure is feel-bad and, under the open-information identity (B12), redundant: income is already public. Now design rule 5.

  • B9 — Severance moved turn order (superseded by B13). The first rider was "smaller share chooses next Rift AND goes first next round" — judged double compensation at the time; turn order was fixed instead. Sam's review reversed this — see B13.

  • B10 — Drought compression vs Last Rites (resolved by B11/B14). The first Last Schism consumed the Reserve faster than base over a starved pool. The revised ending consumes a roughly doubled pool at 1.5/turn for a ~40% third act — the contact with Last Rites is now mild and favourable (longer Droughts serve both modules).

  • B11 — Drought starvation (Sam's review, 2026-07-17). The Reserve is fed only by the binding (2 cards) and TF/PT discards — and TF is already rare at 2p — while the first draft shortened the main game and consumed the Reserve faster: a vanishing Drought. Fix: the Tithe — each Rift the severer sets 1 revealed card into the Arcane Reserve. ~+1/round roughly doubles the Drought pool, makes part of it known and player-curated (deny-now-contest-later), and reads thematically for free (severing spills magic). Necessity check (Sam's challenge, 2026-07-17): a no-tithe arm was modelled — the Drought falls to ~1.7–2.3 rounds (below the already-short base ~2.5–3.5) unless Drought cuts thin to sever-2, the degenerate cut B14 killed. The tithe is load-bearing. Full tithe is not overcompensation in absolute terms: +0.9–1.3 Drought rounds over base (~4 rounds, 37–40% share — the share inflation is mostly the shorter main game). Retreat knobs if the ending drags at the table (details in the Drought-share open question): every-other-Rift tithe (Drought pins to base length ~3.4 rounds with sever-3 cuts everywhere; costs a small rhythm rule) or no tithe + Drought reveal 2 (one-line retreat, base-parity length, but thin endgame cuts — the B14 objection applies to the phase where the cut matters most). The model behind all B11/B14 numbers is a live-formula workbook: Schism Clock Model.xlsx (this folder) — deterministic arithmetic, not simulation; every input, equation, and assumption is a cell you can edit and watch recalculate.

  • B12 — Open-information identity (Sam's review, 2026-07-17). The first draft claimed to protect "the hidden hand" while making all Rift income public — eroding the very asset it cited (only the initial deal and cast draws stay blind). Sam's call: don't patch it, lean in — the Schism is an open-information duel; depth comes from acting on the read, not from re-hiding. The veiled-core fix (a face-down component in every Rift) was offered and rejected. Consequence noted for Veiled Power (cross-module notes).

  • B13 — Severance carries initiative (Sam's review, 2026-07-17; reverses B9). Separate severance order and play order read as two rotating states — confusing. And the benefit of playing first under the Schism is small (no Collection takes exist; it's first Array access for cast exchanges plus the end-boundary turn), so coupling doesn't double-compensate. Fix: one token — the holder chooses the shares and leads the round.

  • B14 — Calibration flipped to reveal-4 (Sam's challenge, 2026-07-17). The Tithe was first grafted onto reveal-3/sever-2 for income parity with base — which guts the premise: the severer has two options, 1/1 freezes the token. The scratch clock comparison (three cast-burn profiles; Source 45; Reserve base ~7) showed reveal-3's only edge was ~0.9 main-game rounds, while both tithe calibrations double the Reserve and balloon a sever-2 Drought to ~50% of the game. Fix on the consumption side, not the tithe: default is reveal 4 / tithe 1 / sever 3, Drought reveal 3 / no tithe / sever 3 — same rich cut everywhere, token in motion every round, Drought ~41% of rounds (base ~29%). Open cost: main game ~6.5 rounds at the mid profile, ~23% fewer casting phases than base — now the headline playtest question.

Open questions (for the model, then the playtest)

  • Is the main game long enough? ~6.5 rounds at the mid burn profile (base ~8.4), ~23% fewer casting phases (B14). Does it still feel like you built an engine, or does the game arrive at the Drought underdeveloped? The headline live-test question. Note the model's assumptions (cast-burn ramp, ~2 TF discards) must be spot-checked against real 2p session logs first — and richer hands plausibly inflate cast burn, shortening it further.

  • Drought share: ~41% of rounds with no Casting phase — third act or slog? Baselines from the phase-length comparison (2026-07-17, mid burn profile; parentheses = realistic 2p Reserve with TF discards ~0): base Drought 3.5 (2.5) rounds; Schism without tithe = 2.3 (1.7) at sever-3 or 3.5 (2.5) at thin sever-2; current tithe-1/sever-3 = 4.5 (3.8), i.e. +1.0–1.3 rounds over base with ~double the per-player Drought income; total game length conserved (~11 rounds) — the tithe moves income into the contested ending rather than extending the game. Knobs if the ending drags at the table: no tithe + Drought reveal 2 (one-line retreat, base-parity length, thin cuts), or half-tithe (parity with rich cuts, tracking fiddle).

  • Income asymmetry: 2/1 rounds hand one player double income; the trailing player's compensation is the token. Does control actually pay off at that exchange rate, or does the card-rich player just win? (The severer prices the cuts — watch whether prices converge sensibly.)

  • Is the Severance too strong? It now carries choice + initiative (B13) — does the holder always take the small share to keep it, locking control? The counter-lever is the severer's tithe + price-setting.

  • Does the tithe read as a decision? If the severer's pick is always obvious (deny the chooser's visible need), it's an auto-pick — fine but shallow; watch whether "deny now vs contest later in the Drought" ever genuinely bites.

  • Array health: with Collection takes gone, does the Array (at base-side size 3) still give Transfiguration enough exchange appeal, or does TF need the Array back at 5 under the box? (TF is already fragile at 2p — don't make it worse.)

  • Tier 2 variance: texture or noise? Kill criteria: players stop planning around Rift cards, or post-game feedback says "swingy". (The Arcane Weather precedent applies — cut the deck, keep the Severing.)

  • Teach: is reveal–tithe–sever–claim genuinely the ~1 minute it looks like, written-rules-only? (Rules-and-teaching thread constraint.)

Scaling

None — 2p only (design rule 1). The line's first count-gated module: the standing "identical at 2–4p and 5–6p" rule is satisfied vacuously (Echo never enters a 2p deck), and the wording stays suit-agnostic to the letter. The index entry carries the count-gating note so the rule's phrasing can be revisited if a second count-gated box ever appears.

Cross-module notes

  • Rival's Gambit (base-side): the box assumes the free 2p mode exists underneath; take-rule and safety valve dormant under the Schism (B1); Source tranches and take-and-bury remain compatible if in use.

  • Trials + Veiled Power (base-side opt-in): mechanically compatible — the Last Schism ends where evaluation begins. But note the B12 tension: at a Schism table hands are mostly public, so Veiled Power has thinner fuel and less bite. Acceptable (both are opt-in layers), flagged for whoever tests the combination.

  • Schools: clean; the natural couples bundle (identity box + duel box). Matchup variety at 2p is Schools' job, not this module's.

  • Last Rites: contact now mild and favourable (B10/B11/B14: a ~40% third act over a deeper, partly-known pool — more Rites windows, and the tithed cards are visible Rite fuel). Still test separately first.

  • Foci & Conduits: no rules contact, but two market rituals per round at 2p (Focus row + Rift) — teach/AP watch if ever stacked.

  • Spell Duels: no contact; the designated answer if post-Schism feedback asks for teeth during turns (Duels T1 on top, later experiment).

  • Null Cabal / Overtones / Conclave / Convergence: no contact possible (wrong counts) or none designed (no scoring, no 16+, no politics).
