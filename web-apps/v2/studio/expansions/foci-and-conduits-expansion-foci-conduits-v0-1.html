Foci & Conduits — v0.1 Rules

A spell is what you built. A Focus is what you built it into.

An exclusive public market of Foci — unique instruments that attach to one spell and upgrade what it does when cast — plus Conduits, links that let one cast echo through another spell. Foci are forged from Essence, refined magic that large spells draw from the Deep Source instead of cards — the refinement layer taxes raw intake exactly the way Cities & Knights commodities do. Two cumulative tiers (Foci alone are a complete module; Conduits add on). Modular, opt-in.

Status: shelf-ready on paper, untested. Written to the engine-builder brief (2026-07-16): the base game has the EB spine (persistent tableau, capacity compounding) but lacks piece differentiation, chaining, a draft arc, and an escalation curve. This module adds all four without touching the base loop. Same-day revision (Sam's direction): the free-claim first draft was strictly clock-accelerating; the Deep Source / Essence layer replaces it (see B6, B10–B12).

Why this module exists — the engine-builder brief

The base game's engine pieces are anonymous: every Conjuration draws, every Transfiguration exchanges, and two players' engines differ only in size and shape, never in function. Genre engine-builders live on unique parts, part-to-part combos, and a market you draft from. This module owns exactly that axis:

  • Differentiation: a Focus makes this Conjuration different from every other one at the table.

  • Draft arc: the Focus row is exclusive and public from turn one — players race to build the qualifying host first.

  • Chaining: a Conduit makes casting spell A ripple into spell B — the first spell-to-spell interaction in the line.

  • Escalation: wide-engine and tall-engine Foci reward casting patterns, not just structures.

Deliberately not delivered: engine→RP conversion. The base game's arc (engine builds structure, structure scores) is the hook, and the engine still dies at the Drought.

The clock bargain (the Cities & Knights move): a raw engine accelerates the Drought — every boosted cast burns extra Source. The counterweight is the Deep Source: large spells can draw refined Essence instead of cards, and Essence is the only way to forge Foci. Upgrading your engine therefore reduces your raw card intake first — commodities instead of resources — and every Essence taken is a Source card spared. The module's clock contact becomes a table-wide choice (burn the world or refine your craft) rather than a side-effect, and DR-HOOK stays amplified: the players decide how fast the world dies.

Fit: why its own module

Considered and rejected as a fold-in:

  • Into The Schools — wrong axis. A School talent is global, dealt identity (modifies all your spells of a kind, from minute one); a Focus is local, built equipment (modifies one host spell you had to construct). Merging would also blow past Schools' one-card teach load.

  • Into Rituals & Seals — closest neighbour; both reward built structure with ongoing abilities. But Seals are non-exclusive rules lenses earned by feats and never touch a specific spell; Foci are exclusive engine parts attached to a host, and the race for them is the point. Rituals owns the personal mastery arc; this owns the contested acquisition arc.

  • Into Overtones — same "exclusive public row" grammar, opposite reward: Overtones races for scarce scoring cards; Foci never score (design rule 1). Prestige vs machinery.

  • Into Spell Duels — Duels owns direct interaction between players; Conduits are interaction between your own spells. No contact.

Throughline owned: the engine arc — unique parts, drafted under contention, chained together. It is the only module in the line that deepens the Casting phase itself (Rituals deepens Learning; the two are complementary bookends).

Design rules (binding on every Focus and Conduit, present and future)

  1. Foci and Conduits never score. No RP, no leans, no evaluation contact.

  2. Nothing touches counters, capacity, or the number of casting/learning actions (the protected economy). Foci modify what a cast does; Conduits add a bounded echo effect, never an extra cast.

  3. Suit-agnostic, count-agnostic wording (the standing compatibility rule). No Focus names an energy.

  4. No values 16+ (Overtones' space), no frequency bands (Spell Duels Tier 3's device), no structural-feat conditions (Rituals' device — Focus requirements are spell-type + size only).

  5. No Focus duplicates or strictly upgrades a School talent or a Seal. Checked line-by-line against both rosters on every roster change.

  6. Max one modifier per effect instance across all modules (shared line, adopted from Rituals' Inner Flame ruling): if a School talent, Seal, and Focus could all modify the same draw or exchange, the owner picks one; the others do not apply to that instance.

  7. Effect-template discipline: every Focus effect is a variation on the base cast-effect templates — draw N, exchange N, source-switch, order/optionality, timing reward — varying numbers and trigger windows only (combination governance rule 4).

  8. Essence buys module content only. It never converts to RP, cards, counters, or anything another module prices — it is not a currency the rest of the line can inflate.

Tier 1 — Foci

Components

  • 8 unique Focus cards (instrument art + requirement + forge cost + effect).

  • ~24 Essence tokens (shared supply).

Setup

Reveal player count + 2 Focus cards face-up beside the Array (all 8 at 6p); return the rest to the box. The row is public from turn one — like the Ritual and Rites rows, the visibility is the expansion: players build toward the Focus they want, knowing rivals can forge it first. Place the Essence supply beside the row.

The Deep Source — tapping Essence

Beneath the ambient Source lies concentrated, refined magic. Only a spell of real scale can reach it — and what it brings up is Essence, not components.

  • Tap: whenever a cast spell of 5+ components grants you bonus draws (its base Conjuration/PT effect, a Focus effect, or a resonance where the cast spell is 5+), you may convert any of those draws into Essence, 1 Essence per forgone draw. A converted draw is never taken from the Source — the card stays.

  • The Collection-phase pick can never be converted (the base income floor is untouched; a player ignoring the module plays exactly the base game).

  • Essence is public, kept in front of you. It is not a card, cannot be stolen or discarded by any current module, and is worth nothing at evaluation (design rules 1 and 8).

Forging, holding, and losing a Focus

  • Forge: during your Learning phase, pay a row Focus's forge cost in Essence (returned to the supply) and tuck the card under a spell in your Spellbook that meets its requirement (the host). No action cost (design rule 2), max 1 Focus forged per player per turn.

  • Exclusive: each Focus exists once. First built, first served.

  • Cap: max 2 Foci per player (1, and no Conduits, while any player-dynamic module — Null Cabal, future co-op — is active; combination governance rule 2).

  • Integrity: the moment the host stops meeting the requirement (Reshape, Unlearn, Sundering, anything), the Focus returns to the row — it recirculates; exclusive supply never sleeps (contrast: Seals go dormant, because they're non-exclusive). Essence is not refunded — the forge is sunk cost; holding the qualifying structure is part of what you paid for (the Rituals principle, priced).

  • Checkable at a glance: the card sits under its host; anyone can verify the requirement right now.

Forge-cost logic: draw-generating Foci prepay their own acceleration (cost ≈ the extra draws of a typical hold); choice-only Foci (Perfect Chord, Crescendo) are cheap entry points; the 6+ premium pair costs most because their hosts also tap Essence fastest.

Tier 2 — Conduits (add after Tier 1 tests clean)

Components

  • 6 Conduit tokens (a two-ended link).

Rules

  • Attach: during your Learning phase, pay 2 Essence and place your Conduit linking two castable spells in your Spellbook, A → B, where B is strictly smaller than A (power flows downhill — checkable at a glance). Directional, max 1 Conduit per player (v0.1).

  • Resonance: when you cast A, B resonates: apply B's cast effect at its minimum template — a Conjuration-type B draws 1; a Transfiguration-type B may make one optional discard-1 → take-1 exchange; a PT-type B does one or the other (your choice). B is not marked as cast; no counter is placed.

  • Bounds: each Conduit resonates once per turn. Resonance never triggers another Conduit and never triggers Foci timing effects (Crescendo/Patient Well count casts, not resonances). No cascades (see B4).

  • Integrity: if the size relation breaks or either end stops being a castable spell, the Conduit returns to your supply immediately.

  • Move: relinking is a new attachment on a later Learning phase (pay the 2 Essence again; only one forge/attach per turn across Foci + Conduits).

Drought handling (both tiers)

All Foci and Conduits are external by definition — casting does not exist during the Drought, so every Focus and Conduit is dormant from the moment the Source empties. They never score, so they simply stop mattering: the engine you built them into must already have been converted into structure. Unspent Essence is likewise worthless in the Drought and at evaluation — the Deep dies with the Source. One line on the box card: "When the Source dies, so do your instruments — spend the Deep before it closes."

Trials riders: none. Foci and Conduits are invisible to the Trials (design rule 1).

Iteration log — bugs found and fixed before v0.1

  • B1 — Capacity-granting Foci cut. First drafts included "while attached: +1 capacity" — the single most EB-flavoured effect and exactly what design rule 2 forbids (the protected economy; same trap as Schools B1 and Rituals B2). Fix: every Focus re-expressed as a cast-effect modifier; capacity growth stays exclusively Enchantment territory.

  • B2 — Ashbinder-shadow Focus cut. "Host 3-card Transfiguration exchanges discard 1 → take 1" strictly duplicated the Ashbinders talent on the host. Fix: design rule 5; the Transfiguration Focus became the Twin Gate (volume, not cost — a different decision).

  • B3 — Refill-choice and placement Foci cut. A "choose the Array refill from top 2" Focus shadowed the Harmonists; a "return the reveal top or bottom" clause on the Long Vein trod on the placement axis the Gravewrights and the Held Note own. Fix: refill-choice cut entirely; the Long Vein's return is deterministic (top).

  • B4 — Conduit cascades banned. The first Conduit draft allowed chains (A→B→C): a three-link turn produced untracked multi-draws, real AP, and a Source-clock blowout. Fix: resonance never triggers another Conduit; one hop, once per turn, one Conduit per player.

  • B5 — Resonance-as-full-cast cut. "Casting A also casts B for free" is a de facto extra cast — a breach of the protected economy in spirit even though no counter moves. Fix: resonance is a fixed diminished template (draw 1 / one optional exchange), not B's real effect.

  • B6 — Clock contact: countered by the Deep Source (revised same day, Sam's direction). The first draft accepted acceleration as on-hook; Sam flagged the counterweight problem and pointed at the Cities & Knights commodity model (cities produce commodities instead of resources, reducing raw production). Fix: the Essence layer — every Essence tapped is a Source draw not taken, and a forge prepays ~3 spared draws before its Focus ever accelerates anything. Net clock contact moves from strictly negative to roughly neutral and, crucially, becomes a player choice (burn or refine). Playtest measure unchanged: turns-to-Drought delta with/without the module, and whether Focus-heavy tables run long instead of short.

  • B7 — Exclusivity vs Overtones' arc. An exclusive public row is Overtones' trigger grammar. Resolved: Foci never score — the race is for machinery, not prestige; the two can share a table because their rewards never overlap. Grammar overlap noted as a line-level watch item (same note as Rituals' fit analysis).

  • B8 — Modifier stacking with Schools/Seals. Deep Current + Depthseekers on one 6+ Conjuration stacked to +4 draws/cast. Fix: design rule 6 (max one modifier per effect instance, owner chooses) — adopted as a shared line-wide rule from the Inner Flame precedent, not a special case.

  • B9 — Enchantment Focus vs the capacity ladder. The Still Flame originally read "cast to draw 1 and the host counts one size larger for capacity this turn" — the second clause touches capacity (rule 2) and creates a phantom-ladder headache. Fix: cut to the draw; the Focus's whole point is letting a passive piece join the engine, which the draw alone delivers.

  • B10 — A physical second deck rejected. The literal reading of "a secondary higher-level source" — deal N cards from the Source into a separate Deep Source deck at setup — was considered and rejected: carving shortens the base clock for everyone before anyone opts in (the tax lands on non-participants), and unspent Deep cards at Drought need a disposal rule with only bad options (lost = feel-bad length variance; shuffled into the Released Reserve = the Drought lengthens because nobody engine-built, which is backwards). Conversion at the moment of the draw delivers the same economy — refined magic instead of raw — with zero new decks and strictly opt-in clock math. The fiction survives intact: the Deep is reached through big spells, not dealt out at setup.

  • B11 — Foci inside the second source rejected. Putting the Focus cards themselves in a face-down Deep deck (the other variant considered) kills the public row — blind-drawing your engine's identity destroys the draft arc and the build-toward-what-you-see promise (open question 1). The row stays visible; the Deep provides the means, never the parts.

  • B12 — Free claiming superseded. The v0.1 first draft attached Foci free on requirement. That made the module strictly accelerating (old B6) and rich-get-richer: the first player to a big host got a free amplifier. The forge tax is the C&K trade — upgrading production reduces raw intake first — and doubles as the catch-up brake (a leader converting draws to Essence is a leader not drawing cards).

Open questions (for the playtest)

  • Does the row shape building? The design's promise is that players construct hosts toward visible Foci. If Foci are only ever claimed incidentally, the draft arc is dead and the module is just eight small buffs.

  • Is the race real at low counts? Count+2 gives 4 Foci at 2p with a cap of 2 each — is that scarcity, or does everyone get what they want? (Watch alongside the 2p Array-contention work — same disease, different organ.)

  • Runaway engine check: the genre risk. Does the first player to a strong Focus+host pair snowball? Measure final-score spread with/without the module.

  • B6 measure: turns-to-Drought delta both ways — does Essence conversion make Focus-heavy tables run long now, and does the table read the burn/refine choice as drama (hook) either way?

  • Essence economy numbers: is 1:1 conversion right, is the 5+ tap gate right (vs 6+), and are forge costs 2/3/4 the right cadence (~2 big casts per Focus)? Measure: turns-to-first-Focus — if the tax makes the module too slow to matter, it's dead weight.

  • Does Essence read at the table? A public token pile telegraphs "she's one short of the Mirror Font" — is that draft tension (good) or does hoarded Essence that dies at the Drought just feel bad?

  • Conduit AP: does resonance tracking slow casting-phase play at 4+?

  • Teach load: Foci add a row + attachment rule; is this teachable in one sentence per card like Schools, or does requirement-checking drag?

Scaling 2–6

The row scales (count+2, all 8 at 6p); nothing else scales. All requirements are spell-type + size (design rules 3–4) — identical text with and without Echo. At 5–6p, richer suits make 6+ hosts (Long Vein, Mirror Font) easier in absolute terms; acceptable per the same precedent as Rituals' Spanning Arc note; flagged for the 5p session.

Cross-module notes

  • Schools: natural pairing — dealt identity chooses which Foci you want (a Depthseeker covets the Mirror Font, not the Deep Current — rule 6 makes them non-stacking). Watch items: does the pairing over-reward Conjuration-heavy builds, and do Depthseekers tap Essence too fast (their +3 draws on 6+ Conjurations are all convertible)?

  • Rituals & Seals: complementary bookends (Learning vs Casting phase). Both put markers on spells — with both active, readability of a spell carrying a Seal chit and a Focus card is a physical-design watch item. Combined attachment cap when stacked: unchanged (2 Seals + 2 Foci) pending a test.

  • Last Rites: Sundering or spending a host returns its Focus to the row — late Rites can deliberately free a contested Focus for a rival; noted, accepted (it's a real decision, not a bug).

  • Spell Duels / Null Cabal: no rules contact. Under Cabal: cap 1 Focus, no Conduits (governance rule 2).

  • Trials: invisible (rider above).
