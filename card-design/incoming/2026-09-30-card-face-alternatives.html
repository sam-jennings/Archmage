Archmage Ascension · energy cards · review of 30 September 2026

Card Face Alternatives

The arcana art is strong and should stay. The reading layer around it has eight problems, and two of them will cause misreads at the table today. Below: what's wrong, four ways to fix it, and every option laid out in a Spellbook, a hand and the Array.

Adopt A now

Arcana+ fixes all eight problems without changing the look. Three of its fixes are toggles playtable.html already has.

Test B against D

Both read better across the table than A. They differ in how far they move from the current mood. Players should pick, not us.

Park C

Vellum is the best result in print and in low light, but it changes the game's whole identity. Revisit only if the pitch direction changes.

What the current face gets wrong

These are the live renderer's own cards (lib/cards.js + art/arcana.js + connectors/beacon.js), drawn on this page by the same code. Click any card to see it turned 180°.

R1
Misread

An upside-down card lies about its value

The corner index survives a 180° turn. The beacon doesn't. Turn a 7 over and its index still says 7, but its orb sits where a 14 would, so a run stops looking like a staircase. The back is symmetric, so nothing tells a player which way up a drawn card is. This will happen.

Fix: put the right-hand orb at the mirrored height. The renderer already does this with the Mirrored connector option (right orb at 21 − v); it just isn't the default.

R2
Hard to read

Your Spellbook only reads from your side

Spells lie in overlapping rows, so only each card's left strip shows. The inverted index sits bottom-right, under the next card. An opponent across the table sees nothing but upside-down numerals, and the hook depends on Spellbooks being read by everyone.

Fix: an index in all four corners, upright at the top and inverted at the bottom. Also an existing toggle (Four corners).

R3
Hard to read

Once cards overlap, energy is colour only

The diagram is the only shape cue, and it's covered in the hand and in the Spellbook. As solid swatches the four base colours hold up under simulated protanopia and deuteranopia (worst ΔE00 22–23). On the card they're thin glowing strokes and a 3.6 mm orb on near-black, where differences shrink a lot.

Fix: the energy icon under every index. Use the official icons in art/energy-symbols-export/; the renderer's Corner glyph toggle draws simpler stand-ins.

R4
Misread · 5–6p

Echo is drawn in two greys, and one of them is the wild's

lib/cards.js colours Echo's index, orbs and label #c8d4e8. arcana.js draws its diagram in #d7d4c8. The first is ΔE00 4 from the wild's #c8d8f8, which is the same colour in practice. The second sits ΔE00 6 from Flux under protanopia.

Fix: one Echo colour, pearl white #f2efe8, and a wild with no hue of its own. This also settles the open question on _review/VISUAL_SYSTEM_with_Echo.md.

R5
Print

Void prints grey

#c060f0 is outside the FOGRA39 coated gamut, the usual European print profile. It comes back as #9d75b0, ΔE00 13. Flux shifts by 5; Radiance, Aether and Echo by 1 or less. Screen proofs and Tabletopia will look richer than the printed deck.

Fix: a printable Void, #9d6fd0 (ΔE00 7 in print), and Flux #1fbcae (2). The cost: Void and Flux drift closer under deuteranopia (22 → 19). The icons cover that.

R6
Hard to read

6 and 9 are unmarked

Both exist in every energy. The centre numeral has no inverted twin, so in the Array a 6 reads as 9 to anyone sitting opposite.

Fix: underline both, everywhere they appear.

R7
Polish

The wild's value column doesn't line up

Normal cards put value v at 42 + 13v units. The wild's column uses 16 + 16v, clamped. Its orbs never sit level with a neighbour's, and both scales still run to 20 although both decks stop at 15.

Fix: one 1–15 scale shared by every card, wild included.

R8
Polish

Low values crowd the corner

At 1 and 2 the orb's halo runs into the index numeral (Radiance 2 above).

Fix: start the scale below the index block.

What already works

The deck reads as one family. Each energy's diagram has its own silhouette. The back is 180° symmetric and gives nothing away. And the beacon is the right idea for a rummy spread: value as height, so a set reads as a level line and a run as a staircase. Every alternative below keeps it.

Four alternatives

All four keep the arcana art and the value-as-height idea. They change what carries the reading layer: the index, the value marker and the materials. A and B stay inside VISUAL_SYSTEM.md. C and D each break one of its rules on purpose.

A

Arcana+

Refine

The chosen design with R1–R8 fixed and nothing else changed. The cheapest option by far.

Changes

  • Mirrored right orb, so a turned card still reads true R1
  • Index in four corners with the official energy icon R2R3
  • Pearl Echo, hueless wild, printable Void and Flux R4R5
  • Underlined 6 and 9; one 1–15 scale, starting below the index R6R7R8

Costs

  • Busier corners: four indices and four icons
  • Players still need to leave about 15 mm of each card showing
  • Glow on black still prints flatter than it looks on screen
B

Instrument Rail

Refine

Both long edges become a calibrated scale. The value is a seal on an engraved rail, with a pointer that runs to the trim, so a run still reads as a staircase when only 6 mm of each card shows. The value moves into a cartouche that reads across the Array.

Gains over A

  • Works in a much tighter overlap: the pointer starts at the card edge
  • The rail's ticks give every orb a reference, so single cards read too
  • Heavier numeral in the cartouche for table-distance reading
  • Closest to the spec's "instrument panel designed by ritual scholars"

Costs

  • The art shrinks by about 15% to fit the plate
  • More line-work; less glow, more engraving
  • A new connector and layout, not just toggles
C

Vellum Plate

Departure · breaks the dark-base rule

The same arcana diagrams printed as ink on light stock. The back stays dark, so face-up and face-down cards can never be confused. The visual system rules out parchment explicitly; this is the case for breaking that rule.

Gains

  • Every ink sits inside FOGRA39 (worst ΔE00 2): what you proof is what prints
  • Dark numerals on light stock: the best contrast in a dim room
  • The wild is the only card with a dark inlay, so it's unmistakable
  • Echo as iron-gall black stays clear of every other ink

Costs

  • Loses the luminous glow that defines the current mood
  • Board, reference cards and web surfaces would need to follow
  • Light stock shows grime; dark stock shows chipped edges instead
  • Blends into a light wooden table (try the Oak surface below)
D

Spine

Departure · breaks "colour lives in the motif"

A solid energy-coloured spine carries everything a player reads in a spread: a big dark numeral, the icon, a value window and notches. A Spellbook row becomes a coloured bar chart. A Conjuration is one colour, a set is a level line of windows, a run is a staircase.

Gains

  • The strongest read at table distance and in low light
  • One-sided on purpose: a turned card shows its spine on the wrong edge, so players fix it
  • The wild's striped spine can't be mistaken for anything
  • The art is untouched; only the layout changes

Costs

  • Less ceremonial; closer to a modern card game
  • 15 mm of every card goes to the spine
  • A player who fans right to left sees only the bare edge

Compare at the table

The same cards in every treatment, at true proportions. Overlapped cards leave a 15.7 mm strip, about what a player leaves when laying out a Spellbook. The Vision filter simulates full-strength colour-vision deficiency on everything in the panels.

Recommendation

  1. Adopt A in the renderer. Set Mirrored, Four corners and Corner glyph as defaults, then patch the rest: official icons, 6/9 underlines, one 1–15 scale for every card, pearl Echo, printable Void and Flux. The F14 hold still blocks print regeneration, so this only changes the renderer until decision-register F1 and F2 close.
  2. Don't swap the Tabletopia deck before the v3.1 confirm/kill. The faces aren't what that session tests, and re-uploading 62 cards costs time you'd rather spend recruiting.
  3. Let players choose between B and D. Print one run and one set in A, B and D on plain paper. Seat two people across the table and have them call out values. Ten minutes, and it fits at the end of any session.
  4. Park C. It's the best option in print and in low light, but it changes the whole identity. It's worth reopening only if the pitch direction moves toward a lighter, classic tabletop look.

Method. Colour differences are CIEDE2000 on sRGB values. Colour-vision simulation uses Machado, Oliveira and Fernandes (2009) at full severity, the same matrices the Vision filter applies. Print checks round-trip each colour through FOGRA39 coated with littleCMS (relative colorimetric). printenbind's actual press profile may differ.

Sources. Current cards are drawn by the live renderer as of 30 September 2026. A–D reuse art/arcana.js for their art and the icons in art/energy-symbols-export/. Card geometry is 252 × 358 units (62 × 88 mm). Indices and value markers use the live renderer's edge inset; B's pointer and D's spine run to the trim on purpose, so a drifting cut can't hide them.

Housekeeping. arcana.js still names the wild palette 'CONVERGENCE'. It isn't rendered, and the retired-term check is case-sensitive, so it slips through.
